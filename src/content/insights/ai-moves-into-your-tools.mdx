February 5 was the loudest enterprise-AI day of the year so far, and almost nobody wrote it up the way it deserves.

**Anthropic shipped [Claude Opus 4.6](https://theagencyjournal.com/claudes-latest-glow-up-opus-4-6-and-sonnet-4-6-land-hard-in-2026/)** with a 1-million-token context window, a 14.5-hour autonomous task horizon, and — most notably — direct integration as an add-in inside **Microsoft PowerPoint and Excel**.

**OpenAI shipped [Frontier](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/05/openai-launches-a-way-for-enterprises-to-build-and-manage-ai-agents/)**, a platform for enterprises to build, deploy, and manage AI agents "like human employees," with identity, access controls, and auditability baked in.

Both on the same day. The pattern behind that coincidence is the story.

## The Shift: From App to Fabric

Until roughly a year ago, using enterprise AI meant opening a new tab. ChatGPT lived at chat.openai.com. Claude lived in its own app. Copilot was a separate surface. You interrupted your work to talk to the AI, then copy-pasted the answer back.

That model is ending. The pattern emerging across every major vendor:

- **AI is moving into the tools you already use.** Opus 4.6 inside PowerPoint and Excel is the clearest signal yet. You don't switch apps — you ask Claude inside the doc you're already editing.
- **Agents are getting identity and governance.** OpenAI Frontier treats agents as employee-class actors with permission scopes, audit trails, and managed deployments. That's the feature set enterprises have been asking for since GPT-4.
- **Integrations are deal-shaped, not product-shaped.** Snowflake and OpenAI announced a major enterprise agreement this week. The battle isn't "which chatbot is best" — it's "whose agent can reach into your data, your docs, and your workflow."

## What This Means for Buyers

If you're evaluating AI tools right now, three practical consequences:

**Audit what's already bundled before buying new.** If your team uses Microsoft 365, Claude and Copilot are either already there or about to be. If you use Google Workspace, Gemini integrations are rolling out fast. You may not need to buy a separate AI product — you may need to turn on the ones you're already paying for.

**Evaluate the integration, not the model.** Every frontier model is close enough in raw capability that the differentiator is where the model lives. A slightly-worse model inside the tool your team uses ten hours a day beats a slightly-better model in a separate tab they open twice.

**Budget for governance, not just licenses.** Frontier and similar platforms exist because enterprise AI is now an identity and access problem, not just a chat problem. Who can spawn agents? What can agents access? How do you revoke them? That's the procurement question for 2026, and it's the one your IT team actually cares about.

## Where This Is Heading

The Opus 4.6-in-PowerPoint moment is not about PowerPoint. It's the start of a period where AI stops being a destination and becomes infrastructure — something that shows up in every app, every workflow, every document, with consistent memory and permissions.

That's good news for most organizations. It's bad news for any vendor whose pitch is "use our separate AI tool instead of the one baked into the software you already own." The ones who survive will be the ones who integrate where the work actually happens.
